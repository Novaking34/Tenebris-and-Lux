== Canonical Introduction

This document establishes the living world-building canon, cosmological ontology,
and historical epic of *Tenebris and Lux* ("Darkness and Light"). 
Conceived in 2014–2018 as the foundational game project of **CreativeWorkzStudio**, 
this is the epic of two primordial relics—**The Shield of Lux** and 
**The Sword of Tenebris**—set within the storied **Kingdom of Sacyra** (pronounced *"saw-kee-ruh"*).
The story is driven by two central protagonists: **Elijah Ro-enheart**, son of a former head
of the Royal Guard, and **Katrina Jaren**, a noble-born daughter navigating royal court expectations
while yearning for the Adventurers' Guild, as the re-awakening **Dark Lord Malakor** rises from the abyss.

== 1. The Primordial Cosmology: Neutrality of the Apex Forces

[quote, The Sacyran Chronicle of Antiquity]
"Light without shadow is a blinding desert that calcifies the spirit.
Shadow without light is a suffocating void where purpose drowns.
The Shield preserves and confines; the Sword severs and liberates.
The relics know neither virtue nor malice—only the soul that wields them."

In the cosmology of *Tenebris and Lux*, the cosmos is governed by two apex forces that
transcend human morality. Neither is inherently good nor evil:

=== Lux (The Apex of Radiance)

* **Ontological Essence**: Manifestation, illumination, cohesion, preservation, and structure.
* **The Dual Nature of Light**:
  - **Virtuous Manifestation**: Wards the vulnerable with impenetrable golden barriers, unmasks dark deceits, preserves cultural knowledge, and inspires steadfast courage.
  - **Tyrannical Manifestation**: Scorches the earth in unyielding glare, blinds those seeking mercy, enforces inflexible order, and petrifies living will into stone.

=== Tenebris (The Apex of Darkness)

* **Ontological Essence**: Mystery, dissolution, rest, severance, and the fertile void.
* **The Dual Nature of Darkness**:
  - **Virtuous Manifestation**: Veils the oppressed from predators, cuts through unholy bonds and tyrant armor, grants restful oblivion to the weary, and pierces through arrogant illusions.
  - **Malevolent Manifestation**: Spreads creeping rot, feeds paranoia, dissolves conscience into nihilism, and serves the destructive malice of the Dark Lord.

=== The Moral Sway of the Wielders

Neither Lux nor Tenebris dictates a character's morality. Good and evil belong entirely to the
**intent, conscience, and actions of the wielder**. In the Kingdom of Sacyra, a knight channeling
the blinding radiance of Lux can act as a cold oppressor, while a warrior wielding the void-quenched
blade of Tenebris can stand as the people's ultimate protector.

== 2. The Twin Relics: Shield of Lux & Sword of Tenebris

Before the crowning of Sacyra's first monarch, primordial celestial smiths forged two sovereign
armaments to anchor the realm against cosmic oblivion:

| **Aspect** | The Sovereign Aegis of Radiance | The Sovereign Blade of the Abyss
| **Physical Form** | A towering kite-shield of burnished solar silver, centered with a living celestial sunstone that pulses with warm golden resonance. | A two-handed greatsword forged from star-quenched obsidian, its edge rippling with deep twilight violet mist and void-tempered steel.
| **Primal Function** | Warding, kinetic deflection, barrier projection, illumination, and stasis shielding. | Severance, armor piercing, shadow manipulation, reality-edge slicing, and mist cloaking.

=== Harmonized Equilibrium

The Shield and Sword were never intended to war against one another; they are complementary halves
of a cosmic balance:
- When wielded in tandem or in synchronized coordination by trusted companions (**The Stance of Equilibrium**), the Shield deflects kinetic onslaught while the Sword opens devastating counter-attack avenues.
- If one relic is wielded without the tempering influence of the other, its bearer risks succumbing to either calcifying stasis or devouring void-sickness.

== 3. The Kingdom of Sacyra: Setting & Society

The **Kingdom of Sacyra** (pronounced *"saw-kee-ruh"*, `/sɔːˈkiːrə/`) is a grand, historic realm
defined by stone bastions, sweeping borderlands, and deeply rooted chivalric traditions.

=== Governance and Courtly Politics

* **The Crown & The Royal Guard**: Sacyra's royal palace commands the central citadel. The **Royal Guard** represents the pinnacle of martial honor and personal devotion to the throne, sworn to protect the realm against external ruin and internal treachery.
* **The Noble Houses**: Hereditary aristocratic families hold vast regional fiefs, supply knights to the royal army, and compete for influence and appointed seats on the prestigious **Royal Court**.
* **The Adventurers' Guild**: An independent, meritocratic brotherhood operating throughout Sacyra's frontier outposts and merchant crossroads. Unlike the rigid court or the ceremonial nobility, the Guild takes on perilous monster contracts, frontier patrols, and ancient ruin expeditions, valuing practical skill and personal freedom above noble pedigree.

=== The Crisis: The Re-Awakening of Dark Lord Malakor

After centuries of dormancy sealed beneath the Abyssal Vaults along Sacyra's furthest borders,
the ancient terror **Dark Lord Malakor** has broken free.

* Malakor is not a personification of Tenebris, but a corrupt mortal arch-sorcerer who twisted dark magic into a tool of catastrophic conquest.
* Seeking to conquer Sacyra, Malakor commands legions of void-blighted fiends, corrupted constructs, and dread vanguard captains.
* His vanguard has already shattered the outer border redoubts, threatening to overrun Sacyra unless heroes arise who can awaken and master the legendary Shield and Sword.

== 4. The Dual Protagonists

The heart of *Tenebris and Lux* is the intertwined journey of two young figures from opposite ends
of Sacyra's social fabric:

=== 1. Elijah Ro-enheart (The Vanguard of Duty)

* **Lineage**: Born into a proud line of soldiers who have served in Sacyra's royal army for generations. His father was a legendary former head of the **Royal Guard**.
* **Role & Archetype**: Frontline combatant, tactician, and stalwart protector. Raised under a strict code of chivalric duty, martial conditioning, and sacrificial defense.
* **Character Dilemma**: Strives to live up to the immense shadow of his father's legacy while questioning whether blind obedience to royal orders truly protects the people of Sacyra when court politics paralyze the realm.
* **Relic Attunement**: Naturally drawn to the **Shield of Lux**, anchoring defensive formations, absorbing enemy assaults, and safeguarding his squad.

=== 2. Katrina Jaren (The Free-Spirited Blade)

* **Lineage**: A daughter of **House Jaren**, one of the most prominent noble families in the Kingdom of Sacyra.
* **Role & Archetype**: Agile skirmisher, perceptive scout, and tactical striker. Possesses natural martial brilliance and a fiercely independent spirit.
* **Character Dilemma**: Raised and groomed since childhood to take a ceremonial seat upon the Royal Court, Katrina suffocates under high-society diplomacy. She secretly longs to break free of courtly expectations and join the **local Adventurers' Guild**, seeking genuine adventure and frontline camaraderie.
* **Relic Attunement**: Naturally resonates with the **Sword of Tenebris**, channeling its fluid, decisive severance to bypass armored juggernauts and cut through enemy flanks.

=== Dynamic Interplay

Together, Elijah and Katrina represent the living synthesis of Sacyra: military discipline paired with adventurer initiative; steadfast defense matched with decisive offense; the golden starlight of Lux intertwined with the twilight edge of Tenebris.

== 5. The Tactical Strategy RPG (TRPG) Paradigm

Unlike standard linear dungeon crawlers, *Tenebris and Lux* is built as a **Tactical Strategy RPG**:

1. **Strategic Grid Battlefield**:
   - Battles take place across multi-elevation grid maps (provinces, castle courtyards, bridges, and mountain passes).
   - Units position according to terrain bonuses, line-of-sight, directional flanking, and movement budgets.
2. **Cinematic Combat Resolution**:
   - When a unit initiates combat against an enemy on the grid, the game transitions into a dynamic **traditional side-scroll / action-based cinematic duel**.
   - Attacks, parries, spell wards, and severance slashes play out with visual weight and player agency before returning to the grid.
3. **Moral Sway Influence**:
   - Dialogue choices, tactical decisions (mercy vs. ruthless execution), and relic usage alter the party's **Moral Sway** (`[-100, +100]`), influencing recruitable companions, kingdom politics, and the story's ultimate conclusion.

== Abstract

This developer log chronicles the foundational provenance and overarching creative vision
of *Tenebris and Lux*, rooted in its 2014–2018 high school inception as the premier game project
of CreativeWorkzStudio. It documents the elimination of placeholder factions in favor of the canonical
**Kingdom of Sacyra**, the introduction of dual protagonists **Elijah Ro-enheart** and **Katrina Jaren**,
and the technical specification of the game's true genre: a **Tactical Strategy RPG (TRPG)** fusing
grid-based tactical maneuvering with cinematic traditional side-scroll / action-based combat resolution.

== 1. Historical Provenance: The 2014–2018 High School Genesis

Before CreativeWorkzStudio evolved into a multifaceted publishing house, *Tenebris and Lux* was its
very first major game in active development.

* **The RPG Maker Era (2014–2018)**: Conceived while the author was in high school learning game design
  and programming. The initial iterations were built using the default offerings of RPG Maker, exploring
  community plugins, and crafting characters through its Character Generator.
* **The Lost Assets & The Blank Canvas**: With no legacy digital assets surviving from that era, the
  project stands not as a nostalgic port, but as a complete ground-up rebirth—realizing the original
  creative vision with the systems engineering maturity, mathematical rigor, and architectural discipline
  of modern custom engine design.

== 2. Narrative Realignment: The Kingdom of Sacyra

During early prototype scaffolding, generic placeholder factions ("The Solar Order" and "The Umbral Covenant")
were inserted to test basic moral sway logic. On October 26, 2026, the creator firmly realigned the canon,
stripping away these synthetic factions to reveal the true world:

=== 1. The Kingdom of Sacyra (`/sɔːˈkiːrə/`)

* The setting is the historic **Kingdom of Sacyra** (pronounced *"saw-kee-ruh"*).
* Sacyra is a structured, chivalric realm anchored by three distinct socio-political pillars:
  1. **The Crown & Royal Guard**: Guardians of legal order, national defense, and the ancestral throne.
  2. **The Noble Houses & Royal Court**: High aristocracy governing regional fiefs and vying for political dominance on the royal council.
  3. **The Adventurers' Guild**: A pragmatic, frontier-based brotherhood operating beyond courtly vanity, answering commoner monster distress calls and exploring untamed ruins.
* **The Existential Threat**: The ancient necromantic sovereign **Dark Lord Malakor** breaking through the Abyssal Vaults, threatening to extinguish all mortal agency.

=== 2. The Dual Protagonists

Rather than a generic lone wanderer, the narrative is anchored by two contrasting protagonists whose
fates, lineages, and martial philosophies intertwine:

== 3. Ludological Evolution: The Tactical Strategy RPG (TRPG)

The game's true mechanical identity is a **Tactical Strategy RPG (TRPG)**. This fundamentally informs
how the simulation engine and presentation viewports are being constructed:

=== 1. The Dual-Layer Combat Loop

The combat loop operates across two synchronized phases:

1. **Strategic Grid Phase**:
   - The battle takes place on a discrete 2D grid representing castles, ramparts, thickets, and void-blighted frontier passes.
   - Units position based on movement ranges, terrain defense bonuses, directional facing (Front, Flank, Rear), and Zone-of-Control (ZOC) restrictions.
2. **Cinematic Combat Resolution (Side-View Action)**:
   - When a unit initiates combat against an enemy target, the game transitions into a dynamic **2D traditional side-scroll / action-based clash**.
   - Attacks play out with timed player agency: precision blocks with the Shield of Lux, poise-breaking shield bashes, and devastating severance slashes with the Sword of Tenebris.
   - Upon duel conclusion, remaining unit health, stagger, and relic resonance seamlessly persist back to the tactical grid.

=== 2. Architectural Implications for the C Engine

Knowing this was originally an RPG Maker project gives us clear architectural directives:
* **Decoupled Data Architecture**: Static definitions (`Units`, `Skills`, `Equipment`, `TerrainTypes`) must exist as clean data tables distinct from session state (`player_state_t`, `grid_t`).
* **Deterministic Event Pipeline**: Interactive map features (gates, switches, dialog triggers) will follow a clean, reentrant command interpreter model.
* **Zero Engine Bloat**: By building this custom in C11 rather than relying on bloated runtimes, our TRPG will maintain sub-millisecond turn updates, instantaneous scene transitions, and absolute softlock immunity.

== 4. Next Milestone Roadmap

2. **Phase 2 (Active)**: Expand simulation state to model dual protagonists (Elijah & Katrina), party data structures, and the foundational 2D tactical grid.
3. **Phase 3**: Implement the cinematic side-scroll combat transition in the presentation layer.
4. **Phase 4**: Expand the tactical campaign across the provinces of Sacyra to confront the vanguard of Dark Lord Malakor.

= Volume III: Ludology & Tactical TRPG Combat Mechanics

== Chapter 15: Dual-Layer TRPG Combat Architecture

*Source File*: `/home/professorseanex/Tenebris and Lux/docs/game_design/tactical_trpg_system.adoc`

[abstract]
== Overview

This document specifies the dual-layer combat architecture of *Tenebris and Lux*:
a **Tactical Strategy RPG (TRPG)** featuring a **grid-based tactical battlefield** coupled with
**cinematic traditional side-scroll / action-based combat resolution**. Conceived during the 2014–2018
RPG Maker genesis of CreativeWorkzStudio, this design bridges grand tactical positioning with visceral,
action-driven 2D engagement.

== 1. Dual-Layer Battle Architecture

The combat engine operates across two seamless, interconnected operational loops:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   PHASE 1: TACTICAL GRID PHASE                         │
│  Tile Movement, Line-of-Sight, Terrain Defenses, Flanking & Squad ZOC  │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │ Target Engagement
                                    ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              PHASE 2: CINEMATIC COMBAT RESOLUTION (SIDE-VIEW)          │
│  2D Action Clashes, Timed Wards, Severance Slashes & Stance Techniques │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │ Resolution & Damage Sync
                                    ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  PERSISTENT SIMULATION STATE UPDATE                    │
│   HP Deductions, Stagger States, Relic Resonance & Moral Sway Deltas   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

== 2. Phase 1: Grid-Based Tactical Battlefield

The overarching strategic layer takes place upon a discrete 2D grid map representing the castles,
frontier passes, and battlegrounds of the **Kingdom of Sacyra**.

=== 1. Grid Topography & Spatial Invariants

* **Coordinate Space**: Discrete integer grid `(grid_x, grid_y)`.
* **Unit Footprint**: Standard units occupy a $1 \times 1$ grid tile; major boss constructs (such as Malakor's Siege Automata) occupy $2 \times 2$ or $3 \times 3$ footprints.
* **Terrain Types & Modifiers**:
  - **Stone Flagstones / Road**: $1.0\times$ movement cost, $+0\%$ evasion.
  - **Ramparts / Elevated Battlement**: $1.5\times$ movement cost, $+20\%$ physical defense, $+1$ attack range for ranged skills.
  - **Frontier Thicket / Rubble**: $2.0\times$ movement cost, $+15\%$ evasion, blocks cavalry charge.
  - **Void Mire (Malakor's Blight)**: $2.5\times$ movement cost, inflicts $-5$ HP decay per turn on non-void units.

=== 2. Movement & Zone of Control (ZOC)

* **Movement Budget**: Units possess a base `movement_tiles` statistic (e.g. Katrina: 6 tiles; Elijah: 4 tiles in heavy armor).
* **Zone of Control**: Moving into an adjacent tile directly forward of a hostile soldier halts further movement for that turn, anchoring frontline formations.
* **Directional Facing**: Units finish their turn facing one of four cardinal directions (`NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST`, `WEST`). Attacking an enemy from their flank grants $+15\%$ critical chance; attacking from the rear guarantees an unguardable strike.

=== 3. Turn Sequencing

* **Dynamic Initiative Order**: Turn execution is governed by unit Agility. Fast skirmishers (like Katrina) act earlier in the round, while heavy vanguard armorers act later with higher defensive resilience.

== 3. Phase 2: Cinematic Combat Resolution (Side-View Action)

When an attacker initiates an assault or when two opposing units clash on the grid, the game seamlessly
transitions into a dedicated **2D Side-View Cinematic Battle Scene**.

=== 1. Visual & Staging Presentation

* The attacker approaches from the left (player units) or right (enemy units) across a dynamic 2D battlefield backdrop matching the current terrain (e.g., Sunken Citadel battlements, ash-strewn frontier).
* Character sprites render with expressive combat animations: Idle combat stance, forward dash, heavy strike, shield block, recoil, stagger, and victory flourishes.

=== 2. Combat Mechanics During Cinematic Resolution

1. **Active Input & Timed Agency**:
   - Rather than passive numbers scrolling past, combat resolution incorporates timed player inputs:
     - **Timed Ward (Lux Guard)**: Pressing the ward button precisely upon enemy weapon contact triggers a **Perfect Deflection**, absorbing 100% of incoming damage and staggering the foe.
     - **Twilight Severance (Tenebris Strike)**: Pressing strike during the forward dash activates a **Deep Cleave**, bypassing a percentage of enemy armor.
2. **Stagger & Poise Meter**:
   - Every battler possesses a **Poise / Stagger Gauge** ($0 \dots 100$).
   - Repeated heavy strikes or shield bashes deplete enemy poise. When poise hits zero, the target enters **Staggered Vulnerability**, leaving them defenseless against critical finishers.
3. **Stance Animation Morphing**:
   - The character's active stance directly dictates their cinematic combat posture and move set:
     - `STANCE_WARDING`: Elijah locks the Shield of Lux into the earth; golden starlight forms a kinetic buckler.
     - `STANCE_STRIKING`: Katrina or Elijah lowers their center of gravity, dragging the Sword of Tenebris as twilight embers trail the blade.
     - `STANCE_EQUILIBRIUM`: Fluid parry-and-counter sequencing utilizing both armaments in rapid harmony.

== 4. Dual-Protagonist Synergy: Elijah & Katrina

The tactical depth of *Tenebris and Lux* emerges from combining the distinct capabilities of Elijah and Katrina:

| **Relic Mastery** | **Shield of Lux**: Projects a 2-tile radius **Sanctuary Aura** on the grid that grants allies $+25\%$ physical defense and immunity to void blight.
| **Cinematic Moves** | *Aegis Slam*, *Radiant Shockwave*, *Bastion Guard*, *Solar Flare Riposte*.

== 5. Persistent State & Moral Sway Feedback

Upon completion of the cinematic resolution:

1. **HP & Wound Conservation**: Remaining unit health updates onto the tactical grid. Fallen units are removed or enter down-but-not-dead states.
2. **Relic Resonance Tracking**: Energy expended (`shield_power`, `sword_edge`) updates in persistent memory (`player_state_t`).
3. **Moral Sway Evaluation**:
   - Choosing to spare routed enemy captains, warding retreating militia, or using non-lethal shield stagger tilts sway toward **Virtue (+)**.
   - Executing fleeing enemies with abyssal severance or utilizing forbidden void arts tilts sway toward **Malice (-)**.
   - Moral sway unlocks distinct strategic faction options across the Kingdom of Sacyra.

== Chapter 18: Continuous Invariant Testing Philosophy

*Source File*: `/home/professorseanex/Tenebris and Lux/docs/game_design/game_testing.adoc`

[abstract]
== Abstract

In classical software engineering, testing focuses primarily on stateless input/output validation,
API schemas, and unit function assertions (`assert(f(x) == y)`). In game development, testing
requires an entirely distinct paradigm: verifying continuous state continuity, economy balance,
softlock immunity, deterministic replay parity, and strict 60 FPS frame-budget headroom.
This document outlines the testing philosophy governing *Tenebris and Lux*.

== 1. The Fundamental Divergence

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Dimension | Traditional Software Testing | Game Development Testing
| **State Nature** | Ephemeral, localized, often stateless | Continuous, cumulative, multi-turn state machine
| **Failure Modes** | Null pointer exceptions, HTTP 500s, syntax errors | Softlocks, economy collapse, degenerate gameplay loops, visual desync
| **Correctness Metric** | Does output match expected schema? | Does the game feel right? Is the state bounded, balanced, and unexploitable?
| **Input Cadence** | Controlled, structured API payloads | Unbounded, chaotic human keystrokes, rapid button mash, simultaneous events
| **Time Budget** | Milliseconds/seconds per request | 16.6 milliseconds total frame budget (60 FPS) with microseconds for simulation
|===

== 2. Core Pillars of Game Development Testing

=== Pillar 1: Dynamic State Invariants & Bounded Continuity

In a game like *Tenebris and Lux*, state variables (`shield_power`, `sword_edge`, `moral_sway`, `threat_hp`)
are constantly mutated across hundreds of player turns. A single off-by-one error or uncapped addition
will cause state drift, resulting in overflow, negative health zombies, or invulnerability exploits.

* **Invariant Axioms**:
  - `0 <= state.shield_power <= 100` must hold after every single action turn.
  - `0 <= state.sword_edge <= 100` must hold even when spamming strikes at zero energy.
  - `-100 <= state.moral_sway <= +100` must never overflow into undefined integers.
* **Diagnostic Tripwires**: The game engine provides `game_audit_state()`, a self-healing diagnostic
  routine that asserts all invariants simultaneously and outputs a detailed diagnostic manifest
  without crashing the game process.

=== Pillar 2: Deterministic Replay ("Golden Master Replay")

Games are emergent systems. If two players—or an automated CI runner—replay the exact same sequence of
commands from the same seed, the end state must be **100% bitwise identical**:

[source,c]
----
// Golden Master Replay Assertion
TEST_ASSERT(run1.shield_power == run2.shield_power, "Deterministic shield");
TEST_ASSERT(run1.sword_edge   == run2.sword_edge,   "Deterministic sword");
TEST_ASSERT(run1.moral_sway   == run2.moral_sway,   "Deterministic sway");
TEST_ASSERT(run1.threat_hp    == run2.threat_hp,    "Deterministic threat");
----

This ensures that gameplay balance, combat resolution, and narrative branching do not drift across
compiler optimizations, operating systems, or refactored engine code.

=== Pillar 3: Softlock Immunity & Degenerate Play Detection

A **softlock** occurs when a player is technically still playing, but has no valid action forward:
- Running out of relic energy during an un-fleeable battle.
- Getting trapped behind a one-way gate with no exit command.
- Inputting malformed or rapid chaotic strings that cause the parser to lock up.

Our automated stress suite executes **1,000 rapid chaotic commands** (combining invalid strings,
rapid stance transitions, and empty tokens), verifying that the game engine rejects nonsense safely,
maintains continuous memory integrity, and never enters a deadlocked state.

=== Pillar 4: Economy & Combat Balance Invariance

Game testing must verify that no single player action is universally dominant (the "dominant strategy" flaw).
If a player can simply spam `ward` forever without consequence, or if `strike` renders all other actions
useless, the game design collapses:
- Warding increases shield power and moral virtue, but deals limited stagger damage.
- Striking inflicts decisive severance damage, but consumes finite sword edge.
- Balancing recovers both relics and parries attacks, but requires timing and resets extreme moral sway.

=== Pillar 5: Frame-Budget Latency Headroom

In standard software, a backend database query taking 50 ms is acceptable. In game development,
a game running at 60 FPS has **only 16.6 milliseconds** to:
1. Poll hardware events and window queues.
2. Execute game simulation logic.
3. Compute spatial audio and particle animations.
4. Render the scene graph to the backbuffer and blit to the display.

Simulation logic must therefore execute in **microseconds**.
In our automated benchmark (`test_gameplay_scenarios`), 10,000 action turns execute in **160 ms**
(**16.02 $\mu\text{s}$ per turn**), consuming less than **$0.1\%$** of a 60 FPS frame.

== Chapter 23: LeTBS Tactical Grid System Specification

*Source File*: `/home/professorseanex/Tenebris and Lux/docs/game_design/demo_baseline/combat_and_letbs_mechanics.adoc`

[abstract]
== Overview

This document formalizes the grid-based Tactical Strategy RPG combat architecture
implemented via Lecode's Tactical Battle System (LeTBS) in the 2017–2018 demo.
It details movement costs, action economy, scope geometries, terrain manipulation,
and turn manipulation invariants.

== 1. Battle Field Architecture & Action Economy

* **Grid Structure**: 2D tile-based tactical grid mapped over encounter maps.
* **Movement Points (MP)**: Consumable per-turn budget determining movement range:
  - Baseline Protagonists & Allies: `move_points: 4`.
  - Heavy Impact Reductions: Heavy Hit reduces target MP by 3 (`move_points+: -3`).
  - Outrider Haste: Light Steps increases target MP by 1 (`move_points+: 1`).
* **Turn Timeline**: Monotonic timeline ordering turns by entity agility (`auto_turn_order_face`).
* **Directional Facing**: Facing impacts defense and positional abilities (e.g., *Phantom Slash* teleports directly behind the target before striking).

== 2. Scope & Area of Effect (AOE) Geometries

The demo configured rich geometric targeting parameters across skills:

[cols="2,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Scope Pattern | Example Skill | Geometric Definition
| `circle(r)` | *Fire* (`Skill 42`), *Heal* (`Skill 72`) | Manhattan or Chebyshev circle of radius `r` centered on the caster.
| `circle(max_r, min_r)` | *Ghost Arrow* (`Skill 79`), *Dagger Throw* (`Skill 169`) | Annular targeting ring; cannot target adjacent cells within `min_r`.
| `line(n)` | *Explosive Mark* (`Skill 76`), *Great Wind* (`Skill 133`) | Cardinal linear projection up to `n` cells from caster.
| `square(r)` | *Angela Move Scope* (`Actor 5`) | Full square grid extent of radius `r`.
| `custom(pattern)` | *Fire Arrows* (`Skill 82`) | Scripted multi-target projectile pattern hitting all visible adversaries.
| `through_obstacles` | *Teleportation* (`Skill 73`) | Ignores intermediate terrain obstacles, requiring only a free destination cell.
|===

== 3. Terrain State Modifications & Dynamic Hazards

Combat encounters feature active environmental manipulation:

* **Volcano Ground (`Skill 47`)**: Lays hazardous fiery embers across multiple ground cells dealing continuous fire damage.
* **Ice Wall / Ice Prison (`Skill 51`, `Skill 52`)**: Summons physical destructible barricades (`HP: 20`) that obstruct enemy line-of-sight and movement corridors.
* **Explosive Mark & Magic Trap (`Skill 76`, `Skill 80`)**: Proximity-triggered grid landmines that detonate upon enemy entry.
* **Burning Tile Spread (`State 110`)**: Dynamic fire propagation expanding with `spread: 100% circle(1) all`.

== 4. Turn Timeline Manipulation

Unique priest techniques alter the flow of tactical battle:

* **Accelerate (`Skill 87`)**: Advances target position along the turn timeline by 100%.
* **Decelerate (`Skill 88`)**: Delays target position along the turn timeline by 100%.
* **Emergency Turn (`Skill 89`)**: Instantly grants an immediate action turn to an allied battler.
* **Extra Turn (`Skill 86`)**: Grants the caster or target an additional action cycle upon turn conclusion.

== Chapter 31: Dual-Frontend Presentation Layer (CLI + Native X11)

*Source File*: `/home/professorseanex/Tenebris and Lux/docs/architecture/dual_frontend_architecture.adoc`

[abstract]
== Abstract

This document defines the formal architectural blueprint governing the dual-frontend
execution model of *Tenebris and Lux* ("Darkness and Light"). Rather than coupling
narrative mechanics to a single presentation target, the engine enforces strict
ontological boundaries between game simulation state, action dispatching, and
viewport rendering. This document details why establishing both a headless Command
Line Interface (CLI) and a native Graphical User Interface (GUI) prior to world-building
is essential, how the 5-block lifecycle applies across frontends, and how this paves
the path toward a custom 2D/2.5D/3D engine.

== 1. The Core Architectural Philosophy

[quote, OmniCode Architectural Invariant]
"Form precedes fullness. Representation must remain sovereign over presentation."

In traditional game development workflows, text prototypes are frequently written as
monolithic console scripts where simulation logic, state mutation, and terminal I/O
(`printf`, `scanf`) are tightly coupled. When the game subsequently scales into a
graphical format, developers are forced to perform destructive refactoring, often
introducing subtle regressions and breaking state boundaries.

*Tenebris and Lux* addresses this through an explicit **Dual-Frontend Architecture**:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Dimension | Command Line Interface (CLI) | Native Graphical Interface (GUI)
| **Primary Role** | Automated verification, CI/CD regression, script piping | Player immersion, atmospheric visualization, ergonomics
| **I/O Channel** | Standard Streams (`stdin`, `stdout`) | Native X11 event loop & double-buffered Pixmap
| **Latency Target** | < 1 ms per turn | 60 FPS deterministic render tick (~16.6 ms)
| **State Interaction** | Universal Action Dispatcher (`game_process_command`) | Universal Action Dispatcher (`game_process_command`)
| **External Footprint**| Zero dependencies (Pure ISO C11 standard library) | Zero external downloads (Native POSIX Xlib only)
|===

=== Universal Action Dispatcher & Intent Emitter

Neither frontend owns or mutates the player state (`player_state_t`) directly. Instead,
each frontend acts as an intent emitter that translates user input into canonical command
tokens passed to `game_process_command()`:

[source,c]
----
void game_process_command(player_state_t *state, const char *raw_input,
    char *out_msg, size_t msg_len);
----

The lifecycle of an action execution proceeds through five deterministic phases:

[source,text]
----
┌─────────────────┐       ┌────────────────────────┐       ┌─────────────────┐
│ Input Source    │  ==>  │ Command Tokenizer      │  ==>  │ Invariant Check │
│ (CLI or GUI)    │       │ (sanitization & parse) │       │ (state bounds)  │
└─────────────────┘       └────────────────────────┘       └─────────────────┘
                                                                    │
                                                                    ▼
┌─────────────────┐       ┌────────────────────────┐       ┌─────────────────┐
│ Presenter Blit  │  <==  │ Action Result Log      │  <==  │ State Mutation  │
│ (stdout or X11) │       │ (chronicle message)    │       │ (lux, room, etc)│
└─────────────────┘       └────────────────────────┘       └─────────────────┘
----

1. **Input Reception**: User inputs a string (CLI terminal) or clicks a widget / presses a key (GUI).
2. **Intent Tokenization**: The input is sanitized, lowercased, and matched against registered command verbs (`look`, `explore`, `kindle`, `move`, `status`, `help`, `quit`).
3. **Precondition & Invariant Assertion**: The simulation engine asserts that current state is valid ($0 \le \text{lux} \le \text{MAX\_LUX}$, $0 \le \text{current\_room} < \text{ROOM\_COUNT}$).
4. **Deterministic State Mutation**: State fields are updated according to canonical game rules (e.g., kindling decrements shards by 1 and increments Lux by 30 up to 100).
5. **Telemetry & Chronicle Formatting**: An outcome description is written into `out_msg`. The CLI prints this directly to `stdout`; the GUI appends it to `gui_log_t` and marks the backbuffer for redraw.

== 2. Native Desktop GUI Architecture: Why Not Web?

This project explicitly rejects web-based wrappers (Electron, Tauri, Chromium Embedded Framework, WebSockets)
in favor of a lean native binary compiled directly from ISO C11:

[sidebar]
****
*The Fallacy of Web Wrappers in Systems Game Development:*
Web architectures introduce non-deterministic garbage collection pauses, bundle
hundreds of megabytes of third-party browser code, and abstract away hardware memory
boundaries. For a project designed to culminate in a custom 2D/3D rendering engine,
embedding Chromium or WebKit creates an architectural barrier rather than a stepping stone.
****

Key technical advantages of the native POSIX X11 approach:

* **Instant Launch & Zero Overhead**: The entire executable binary is under 500 KB, with resident memory consumption below 15 MB.
* **Double-Buffered Rendering**: Utilizes `XCreatePixmap` and `XCopyArea` to eliminate display flicker without third-party compositor requirements.
* **Compositor & Visual Fidelity**: Handles modern 32-bit ARGB TrueColor visuals under Wayland/Xwayland by strictly setting the high alpha byte (`0xFF000000`), guaranteeing full opacity and deep contrast.
* **Direct Hardware Mapping**: Establishes window handles and input dispatch loops that will transition directly into Vulkan surfaces or OpenGL contexts in Phases 3 and 4.
* **Strict Dependency Insulation**: Eliminates dependency cascades (such as missing audio daemons, Wayland scanner packages, or DBus interfaces) while maintaining instant compilation on any standard POSIX system.

=== Double-Buffering & Render Synchronization

To achieve flicker-free rendering without tearing, `src/gui.c` implements offscreen
double-buffering:

[source,c]
----
// Allocate backbuffer pixmap matching the exact depth of the root window
backbuffer = XCreatePixmap(display, window, GUI_WINDOW_WIDTH,
                           GUI_WINDOW_HEIGHT, window_depth);

// 1. Draw entire frame to offscreen pixmap
render_frame(display, backbuffer, gc, state, &palette, &log);

// 2. Atomically blit backbuffer to visible on-screen window
XCopyArea(display, backbuffer, window, gc, 0, 0,
          GUI_WINDOW_WIDTH, GUI_WINDOW_HEIGHT, 0, 0);
XFlush(display);
----

=== 1.2 The Diagnostic Severity Spectrum

[cols="1,1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Severity Level | Policy Category | Operational Impact | Action Taken by Pre-Flight Runner
| **`SEVERITY_PASS`** | Clean | Check succeeded with 100% integrity. | Logged as verified.
| **`SEVERITY_INFO`** | Informational | Capability discovery (e.g. CPU features). | Logged in telemetry.
| **`SEVERITY_WARNING`** | Non-Critical | Minor variance (e.g. timer resolution > 100ns). | Emits warning notice; boot proceeds normally.

=== 1.3 Expected Pre-Flight Engine States

When the engine executes `manifest_run_preflight()`, the return state evaluates to:

1. **`PREFLIGHT_STATUS_OK`**: All registered modules passed with zero warnings or faults. Full engine launch permitted.
2. **`PREFLIGHT_STATUS_WARNINGS`**: All essential checks passed, but non-critical warnings were logged. Engine launches normally with notices displayed.
3. **`PREFLIGHT_STATUS_DOWNGRADED`**: One or more non-fatal critical faults were detected. The engine executes a fallback transition (e.g. dropping from GUI to CLI mode) and continues running in safe mode.
4. **`PREFLIGHT_STATUS_HALT_FATAL`**: One or more fatal core invariants failed. The engine halts immediately and refuses to launch.

Under modern Linux compositors (such as Wayland/Xwayland), windows frequently provide 32-bit ARGB

[source,c]

static unsigned long alloc_color(Display *d, int screen, unsigned int rgb_hex)
{

    // Map RGB components...
    if (XAllocColor(d, DefaultColormap(d, screen), &col)) {
        if (DefaultDepth(d, screen) == 32 || (col.pixel & 0xFF000000) == 0) {
            return col.pixel | 0xFF000000; // Force full opacity
        }
        return col.pixel;
    }
    return 0xFF000000 | rgb_hex;
}

== Abstract & Engineering Intent

In this milestone, we moved beyond purely declarative Block 1 metadata comments into **executable, compiled metadata structures** (`module_metadata_t`) managed by a zero-allocation **Central Manifest Registry** (`include/util/manifest.h`, `src/util/manifest.c`). == 1. The Core Problem: Loose Bolts & Silent Corruption

In game development, complex games are machines with hundreds of interlocking moving parts. A subtle defect in a foundational layer—such as a non-monotonic clock, an integer overflow on damage saturation, an unchecked negative rectangle dimension, or an unbuffered string copy—can propagate upward, manifesting as elusive micro-stutters, physics drift, state desynchronization, or catastrophic runtime crashes.

Rather than waiting for the game loop to execute before identifying structural anomalies, the engine must perform systematic pre-flight verification on startup, classifying faults along a graded severity spectrum:
* **Warnings (`SEVERITY_WARNING`)**: Log notices (e.g. coarse sleep timer resolution); launch proceeds normally.
* **Critical Faults (`SEVERITY_CRITICAL`)**: Subsystem offline, but engine core intact (e.g. no `$DISPLAY` for X11 GUI); downgrade gracefully to safe mode (terminal CLI).
* **Fatal Violations (`SEVERITY_FATAL`)**: Core invariants broken (e.g. non-monotonic time, arithmetic bounds failure, corrupt initial state); halt the engine immediately with a detailed diagnostic manifest.

== 2. Architectural Implementation

=== 2.1 Compiled Metadata Structures

Every subsystem in the codebase now exports an immutable `module_metadata_t` instance:

    struct { uint8_t major, minor, patch; } version;
    const char *description;
    size_t invariant_count;
    const char *invariants[MANIFEST_MAX_INVARIANTS];
    diag_result_t (*preflight_check)(void);
} module_metadata_t;
----

1. `core.util.manifest`: Self-audits registry capacity limits, duplicate registration rejection, and severity escalation rules.

3. `core.util.math`: Tests clamp bounds, saturating add/sub overflow safety, division-by-zero defense, and nudge directionality.
4. `core.util.geom`: Tests negative dimension clamping, boundary point containment, AABB overlap reflexivity, and viewport bounds containment.
5. `core.util.string`: Tests buffer safe truncation null-termination, NULL pointer resilience, and whitespace trimming edge cases.
6. `sim.engine.core`: Tests initial relic state integrity, resonance saturation ceilings, deterministic stance state transitions, and diagnostic tripwires on corrupt states.
7. `gui.viewport.x11`: Tests button layout non-overlap and asserts window bounds (`gui_audit_layout`), while checking X11 display availability (`gui_is_available`). If `$DISPLAY` is unconnectable, emits `SEVERITY_CRITICAL` to trigger automatic CLI safe mode fallback.

== 4. C11 Engine Rebuilding Roadmap

With the historical baseline fully documented, the path forward for our native
C11 implementation is clear, disciplined, and rigorous:

1. **Layer 0 Core Expansion (`include/util/`, `src/util/`)**:
   - Align `time_utils` to faithfully model the 24-hour calendar cycle driven by
     *Grandma's Watch*.
   - Expand `geom_utils` to support discrete grid-cell raycasting and LeTBS
     range shapes (`circle`, `line`, `square`, `through_obstacles`).
2. **Layer 1 Simulation Expansion (`include/sim/`, `src/sim/`)**:
   - Reconstruct the tactical battle state machine supporting 5-member party
     formations and enemy AI routines.
   - Implement the moral sway continuum engine (`[-100, +100]`) tracked by the
     *Compass of Morality*.
3. **Layer 2 Viewport & Presentation (`include/gui/`, `src/gui/`)**:
   - Render the 2D tactical grid with movement overlays, Zone of Control, and
     turn order timelines.
   - Implement the dialogue presentation window capable of rendering branching
     scripts with character portraits.
4. **Layer 4 Automated Verification (`tests/`)**:
   - Author invariant test suites verifying deterministic grid movement,
     damage formula parity, and softlock immunity under all quest choice trees.
